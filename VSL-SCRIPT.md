**Runtime target:** 9–11 minutes at a measured pace (~140 words/min).
**Delivery:** Arun to camera. One location, seated, quiet room, warm low light. No music for the first 30 seconds — let the silence work. No logo intro; the first frame is Arun speaking.
**Tone:** calm, direct, unhurried. Never salesy. You are the man who has been through it, speaking to the man who is in it.

You get up. You work. You answer the messages. And then the evening comes, and the flat is silent, and you realise you've been performing all day.

Nobody made a film for that. So I did.

Give me ten minutes. I'm going to tell you why you're still stuck, why the usual advice hasn't worked, and exactly what rebuilding looks like when it's done properly.

## 2. The pain, named precisely — 0:35–2:15

[Slightly slower. This section earns the right to everything that follows.]

Let me tell you what I know about where you are — not because I've read about it, but because I've lived it.

I know about the 3am ceiling. The conversation with her that you're still having in your head, months after she stopped being in the room.

I know that the friends went quiet. Not because they don't care — because they don't know what to say to a man whose life has come apart, and men don't have the language for it. So they say "let me know if you need anything," and then it's just you.

I know that you can hold a meeting, hit a deadline, negotiate a contract — and then sit in your car in the drive for twenty minutes because you can't face the front door.

I know the questions underneath it. *Who am I, if I'm not her husband? What did I miss? How do I face my kids as half a household? Am I going to be one of those men who never comes back from this?*

And I know the sentence you've said to yourself more than any other: **"I should be over this by now."**

You shouldn't. Not without a way through. Divorce isn't an event you get over. It's a demolition. And nobody hands you the plans for the rebuild.

---

## 3. The false solutions — 2:15–3:30

Now — you've probably already tried the standard prescriptions. Let's be honest about them.

**"Time heals."** Time passes. That's all it does. Men five years out are still carrying it — the untreated version doesn't fade, it calcifies. It shows up as the edge in your voice, the cynicism about women, the flinch when someone gets close.

**The grind.** Gym, work, cold showers, discipline. Good — genuinely, keep the gym. But you can get a strong body and a full calendar and still be hollow at the centre, because you've built armour, not a man.

**The apps, too early.** Dating before you've rebuilt means you bring the wreckage with you. You either cling to the first warmth you find, or you sabotage it. Either way, you repeat the pattern — with interest.

**And therapy.** Therapy has real value, and if you're in genuine crisis you should be there and I'll tell you so myself. But therapy mostly looks backwards — it processes. What most men in your position are missing is the *forward* half: who am I building, to what standard, and in what order?

None of these fail because you're weak. They fail because they each address one piece of a total demolition.

ON SCREEN: `TIME PASSES. IT DOES NOT REBUILD.`

## 5. The mechanism — the four-stage path — 5:00–7:15

[Energy lifts slightly. This is the "perceived likelihood" engine — the structure is the proof.]

Remane is a private, one-to-one coaching practice built on one idea: **you rebuild in stages, in order, with structure.** Not vague chats. A curriculum — a weekly session with me and precise work between sessions, every week having a purpose.

Four stages.

**Stage one — Recovery.** We steady the ground. The rumination loop, the sleep, the anger, the story you're telling yourself about what happened. Most men feel the ground stop moving in the first three to four weeks — not fixed, but no longer freefall. That matters, because you can't build on ground that's still shaking.

**Stage two — Reconstruction.** The real work. Who you are now — not who you were as a husband, not who you were at 25. Your standards, your habits, your finances, your friendships, your presence. This is where the armour comes off and the actual man gets built.

**Stage three — Re-entry.** The world again. Social confidence. Meeting women — not to grab the first lifeboat, but with discernment: knowing your ideal partner, your boundaries, and what you will no longer tolerate — including from yourself.

**Stage four — Relationship Mastery.** Because the goal was never just to survive the divorce. It's to be capable of the relationship you actually want next time — and to never again outsource your worth to it.

Every stage has a written curriculum behind it. Every week has a session and a piece of work. You will never be left wondering what to do next — that's my job.

## 8. Objections, answered before you ask — 9:00–10:15

[Direct, almost list-like. This section should feel like Arun reading the viewer's mind.]

Now, I know what's in your head right now, so let me answer it.

**"I should be able to do this myself."** Every capable man thinks this — I did. But look at your own life: everything you've ever done at a high level, you did with structure and someone in your corner. A trainer, a mentor, a board. This is the one area men insist on doing alone, and it's why it takes them years.

**"How is this different from therapy?"** Therapy processes the past; this builds what's next. They're complementary — some of my clients do both. But a therapist won't take you through re-entering the world, dating with discernment, and mastering the next relationship. That's this.

**"A thousand pounds a month is a lot."** It is. Now run the other number: what does a lost year cost you? In your career while you're running on empty. In your health. In what your kids see when they look at you. In marrying the wrong woman because you were lonely rather than ready. Against that, this is the cheapest thing on the list.

**"How long until I feel different?"** Honestly: most men feel the first real shift inside a month. The full rebuild is measured in months, not weeks — anyone who promises you faster is lying to you.

**"Will anyone know?"** No. Discretion is built into every part of this practice. It's one of the reasons men choose it.
